## Victor, 39 — cand-1
> I know every maitre d' in this city and at least half of them like me back. Recovering publicist. Ask me where to eat and clear your evening.

**Date 1 — a restaurant with no sign on Mott**

The host greets him by first name and yours arrives moments later, learned from somewhere, said correctly. No menus come; food simply starts appearing, and the chef sends out something not on offer with a wave through the kitchen door. On the single flight up to the mezzanine he goes quiet, one hand on the rail, breathing through it mid-sentence, and picks the sentence back up at the top.

**Date 2 — a gallery opening you'd never have gotten into**

He moves you through the room like he's dealing cards — 'this is the man I was telling you about,' to people he was plainly not telling about you, except that they all seem briefed — and you can feel the room re-price you upward in real time. He watches each introduction until it takes, then moves on. When the dancing starts in the back he waves you toward it and holds your drink, beaming, lightly sweating from the standing.

**Date 3 — his apartment, an old building on the park**

The doorman, the porter, the widow on nine — he knows everyone's name and their kids' names, and in the elevator he tells you, quietly, that rooms used to be very hard for him, and that all this welcome is something he built out of an unwelcome decade. You understand suddenly that the doors he opens are the doors he once stood outside of, and that he's handing you the keyring. The elevator is out for service on the way down; he stops at the third-floor landing and makes a bit of the view, and you both let him.

## Noah, 28 — cand-4
> Climbing instructor, sunrise person, allegedly good hugger (source: everyone). I will plan you a perfect day for eleven dollars.

**Date 1 — golden hour at a spot only he knows**

He plans a date that costs nothing and beats every dinner you've been bought this year: a thermos of good coffee, a western-facing rock in the park he calls his office, the light arriving on schedule like he'd booked it. He notices you favoring your bad shoulder and adjusts where you sit before you've said a word. At the coffee cart afterward he counts out eleven dollars in singles, exact, a moment longer than comfortable.

**Date 2 — lentils at his place, four roommates through the wall**

His room is a bed, a milk-crate bookshelf, and forty feet of climbing rope coiled like a sleeping animal, and he cooks you one perfect cheap meal in a kitchen he shares with four people. You mentioned a dessert once — some restaurant's fourteen-dollar version — and he has made it from scratch, slightly wrong and much better. Through the wall, two of the roommates are discussing the rent math, and he talks a little louder, and you both pretend that worked.

**Date 3 — the night the wedding comes up**

Kissing him feels like being chosen by something with excellent judgment, and afterward he holds you with his whole attention, like there's no clock anywhere in the world. You float the wedding in October — your friends, a flight, a hotel — and he says, plainly, without shame, 'I can't afford that, it's not a secret, it's just math.' He says it the way other men say their job titles, and you lie there doing arithmetic against your own salary and hating that you're doing it.

## James, 34 — cand-7
> Quant, early on some things that worked out. New-ish to the city, learning it one obsession at a time. Currently: bread, Mandarin, you, potentially.

**Date 1 — an omakase counter, booked like it was nothing**

The reservation everyone you know brags about getting simply exists for him, and the check never visibly arrives — money around him has no friction, no gesture, no weather. He asks about your work and then asks the question underneath it, the one you'd been circling for a year, and takes notes with his eyes. When the sommelier presents the bottle, he looks to you for the approval, not to James.

**Date 2 — your friend's birthday, his first room of your people**

He brings the right wine and the wrong everything else — stands a half-step outside every circle, unintroduceable until you do it, and no one asks him a single question all night, which he doesn't seem surprised by. From across the room you watch him study the bookshelf like a man preparing for a quiz that isn't coming. In the cab he says your friends are wonderful and means it, and when you apologize for them he seems honestly confused about what for.

**Date 3 — his apartment, a glass tower over the river**

The place is beautiful and anonymous, like wealth before anyone's spent personality on it, and the sourdough he's been obsessing over is, irritatingly, excellent. When your card debt comes up — you didn't mean for it to — he offers to clear it the way other men offer to get the next round, and you feel the vertigo of the door, how much is on the other side, how much he wants you through it. Later you try to picture the wedding and find that his side of the aisle is the part you can't fill in.

## Gregory, 37 — cand-8
> Estate lawyer. I own the brownstone and I made the gravy. I'm told I laugh a beat late; I'm working on it, slowly, with no urgency.

**Date 1 — an old steakhouse, his usual booth**

He's kind to the staff in the practiced way of a man who tips in cash and remembers surgeries, and the bill is handled somewhere offstage, invisibly, the way certain steady money moves. He tells you about the building he grew up poor across the street from and bought twenty-two years later — not as a flex, but the way you'd describe a wound that finally closed. Your best joke of the night lands; he laughs a full beat late, honestly, like the joke had to clear customs; and when you suggest walking the eight blocks after, he suggests the bus.

**Date 2 — the climbing gym, with your friends**

He comes because you asked, sits out the climbing with everyone's coats in his lap, and looks happy — actually happy — holding four jackets and learning your friends' names for later use. Since your one mention of it two weeks ago, his kitchen has stocked your tea; there is a guest robe in your size with the tags still on; 'let me,' he keeps saying, and you're beginning to understand it means every logistical problem you have could simply stop existing. From the mat you watch him watch you climb, and he never once checks his phone.

**Date 3 — the cabin upstate, off-season**

He insists on carrying the firewood himself and arrives at the porch red-faced and winded, breathing like a man who has decided not to discuss it, and finishes the job in three trips where you'd have done one. Over dinner he says, shy, looking at the stove, that the deed up here has room for a second name, and the sentence is so earnest it makes the room hot. You realize he has never once made you laugh — and that he has caught every single thing you've ever dropped.
